# Method

## What this project is trying to show

The project should make class segregation around higher education visible in ordinary life. Menu prices, luxury services, and student-oriented rents are useful because they show what businesses and landlords believe the surrounding market can bear.

They are indicators, not standalone demographic evidence. The larger claim—college is disproportionately organized for and governed by wealthy people—requires several kinds of evidence that reinforce one another.

| Layer | Useful evidence | What it can establish |
|---|---|---|
| Consumption | menus, retail, services | the affluent market businesses expect |
| Housing | listings, leases, per-bedroom rent | the resources required to live near campus |
| Access | enrollment by family income, aid, debt | who attends and on what terms |
| Labor | campus and local wages | contrast between consumers and workers |
| Control | trustees, donors, developers, property | who governs and profits |
| Displacement | evictions, tax base, land purchases | effects on existing residents |

## Recording an observation

1. Prefer the business, landlord, university, or government page as the primary source.
2. Record the exact advertised price and basis: each, meal, bedroom, unit, or month.
3. Record the date. Never silently overwrite an older price; add a new observation.
4. Preserve a screenshot or archived link where lawful and practical.
5. Measure proximity consistently. Walking distance and route are better than “near campus.”
6. Mark unconfirmed recollections as `lead`.
7. State narrowly what the source supports.

## Avoiding bad inference

Do not photograph or identify individual students merely to guess their wealth. Do not treat everyone in a college town as a student. Do not claim that the presence of one expensive restaurant establishes the class composition of an institution.

The strongest presentation is a paired local record: an expensive item or apartment alongside student family-income data, worker wages, and rent burden from the same place and period.
