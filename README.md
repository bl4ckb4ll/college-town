# College Town

Track the visible political economy of elite college towns: luxury consumption next to campuses, housing costs, wages, displacement, and who can afford to attend.

The motivating observations are deliberately concrete:

- oysters sold around the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor;
- sandwiches approaching $30 around Brown in Providence;
- students living in apartments costing roughly $2,500 per month.

A nineteen-year-old eating oysters while living in a $2,500 apartment is more legible than an abstract tuition statistic. But an anecdote is not, by itself, proof that college is “for and by the one percent.” This repository preserves the visceral observations and connects them to harder evidence: family income, admissions, tuition, rent, wages, ownership, and displacement.

## Evidence rules

Every claim should retain:

- place and nearby institution;
- business or property;
- item and advertised price;
- source URL;
- date observed;
- distance or relationship to campus;
- archived copy or screenshot when legally practical;
- status: `lead`, `verified`, `stale`, or `disputed`;
- a note stating exactly what the evidence establishes—and what it does not.

Prices change. A search snippet is a lead, not durable evidence. Do not infer a customer's age, student status, income, or class position from appearance.

## Layout

- `observations.csv` — comparable index of claims
- `places/ann-arbor.md`
- `places/providence.md`
- `METHOD.md` — standards for turning striking examples into an argument

## Initial research targets

1. Verify a current oyster menu within walking distance of the University of Michigan.
2. Identify and verify the specific Providence sandwich priced at or near $30.
3. Preserve current listings for apartments marketed to students at or above $2,500/month.
4. Pair these with institutional enrollment-by-family-income data, local wages, rent burden, and university real-estate expansion.
